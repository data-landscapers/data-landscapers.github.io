<div class="about-intro__text">
  <p><strong>Data Landscapers</strong> is a project dedicated to data governance and digital transformation in Africa. It is designed and developed by Bill Anderson supported in an advisory capacity by Bernard Sabiti.</p>

  <p>Bill and Bernard began working together in 2014, joining up district-level data in Uganda. Their series of blogs <a href="/assets/pdfs/2015-adventures-datarev-1.pdf">“Adventures in the Data Revolution”</a> captured an approach to bottom-up data development that they have applied ever since. They developed a data landscaping methodology that was put into practice working with UNICEF in Zimbabwe, Uganda, Nigeria, Bangladesh and South Sudan. Lessons from this work were synthesised into two widely read discussion papers – <a href="/assets/pdfs/2021-data-side-of-lnob.pdf"><em>The data side of leaving no one behind</em></a> and <a href="/assets/pdfs/2022-data-disharmony.pdf"><em>Data disharmony: how can donors better act on their commitments?</em></a>. See the <a href="/portfolio/">portfolio</a> for a collection of their work.</p>

<div class="cv-section__label">Corpus</div>
  <p>Back in the 1980s Bill worked as head of data processing for the ANC's military intelligence department. A complete set of South African telephone books was his internet. From them he mapped military and police infrastructures, strategic oil dumps, infiltration routes. Since then he has been fascinated by the potential of open-source intelligence. Today's internet is both a treasure trove and a minefield as are the fast-developing AI tools and post-Google search engines. Corpus is a proof-of-concept exercise to exploit these developments and deliver an automatically driven research resource for everyone working on digital transformation in Africa. It is a repository of summarised and indexed news and documents updated daily.</p>
</div>

<div class="cv-section__label">About Bill</div>

<div class="about-intro">
  <div class="about-intro__text">
    <p>I am an experienced systems architect with expertise in the politics, administration and technology of digital transformation, data governance policy, and data standards. My particular focus is on Africa's digital sovereignty — the struggle to ensure that the continent's data infrastructure is owned, governed and controlled by Africans.</p>

    <p>I spent fourteen years at <a href="https://devinit.github.io/" target="_blank">Development Initiatives</a>, first as data standards advisor and technical lead of the <a href="https://iatistandard.org/en/" target="_blank">International Aid Transparency Initiative (IATI)</a>, then as data ecosystems programme lead. DI went into liquidation in October 2024, and I now operate independently through Data Landscapers Ltd.</p>

    <p>I am a veteran of the South African liberation struggle — conscripted into the apartheid army, I deserted, testified to the United Nations on its atrocities, and joined the African National Congress. That experience shapes everything I do. Digital colonialism is real. The antidote is African-led infrastructure, standards and policy.</p>

<div class="cv-section__label">About Bernard</div>

<div class="about-intro">
  <div class="about-intro__text">
    <p>I have 15 years’ experience researching and managing projects on Africa’s data revolution, data governance, and digital transformation. I served as DI’s man in Africa for 10 years as a senior partnerships and engagement advisor, a role in which I built formal relationships with several government ministries, departments and agencies on strengthening data systems, digitising government services and enabling digital and data interoperability.</p>

    <p>I have extensive knowledge of the digital transformation landscape in Africa, the political economy of data and digital technology on the continent, and the role of local agency in leveraging digital tech ecosystems to bolster social impact. I have worked with and built close partnerships with many African institutions including the African Union, AfDB, Africa CDC and many local and international NGOs.</p>

    <p>I am currently working for the African Population and Health Research Centre in Nairobi where I lead projects on data systems and data governance.</p>

    <p>I support Data Landscapers’ work because I am a passionate promoter of digital public goods and the transformative power of digital technology in Africa and have promoted its adoption in many African countries from Zimbabwe to Uganda.</p>
