# The Google–AfCFTA Secretariat Relationship: History, People, and Agreements

## Overview

The relationship between Google and the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) Secretariat has evolved gradually since approximately 2022, growing out of Google's broader engagement with pan-African institutions including the African Union Commission (AUC) and the UN Economic Commission for Africa (ECA). The partnership culminated in the launch of the AfCFTA Digital Inclusion &amp; Entrepreneurship Programme (ADIEP) in late 2025. Crucially, **there is no publicly documented formal Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) or bilateral agreement between Google and the AfCFTA Secretariat itself**. Their collaboration has been structured through programme-level partnerships rather than a treaty-style legal instrument—unlike the formal MOUs the AfCFTA Secretariat has signed with the United States, UNDP, UNCTAD, ECA, and others.

***

## Early Engagement and Origins (2022)

### Google's Pan-African Pivot

The roots of the Google–AfCFTA relationship trace back to Google's increasing institutional engagement with African continental bodies. In **February 2022**, Google CEO **Sundar Pichai** addressed the African Union Summit business forum, announcing a $1 billion investment commitment to support Africa's digital transformation over five years. In his address, Pichai specifically referenced the African Continental Free Trade Area as a mechanism that "will boost intra-African trade and generate investments in infrastructure," signalling Google's strategic interest in the AfCFTA framework.[^1][^2]

### Creation of the Pan-African Institutions Role

Shortly after the AU Summit, Google created a new, dedicated position to connect with pan-African institutions such as the AUC, the UN Economic Commission for Africa, and the African Development Bank. **Pren-Tsilya Boa-Guehe**, a former U.S. diplomat who had worked at the U.S. Mission to the African Union, was appointed to this role. She relocated to Kenya and began building out Google's policy portfolio with these institutions.[^3]

Boa-Guehe explicitly identified the AfCFTA as a key policy area. In her own words, she noted that "the AU's continent-wide policy initiatives can help to accelerate Africa's digital transformation through the AU's Digital Transformation Strategy and the African Continental Free Trade Area". She described the AfCFTA as a platform to "leapfrog by using technology as a driver of economic growth".[^3]

### Mombasa Policy Discussions (March 2022)

One of Boa-Guehe's first major engagements was a trip to **Mombasa, Kenya in March 2022** for a policy discussion specifically focused on the AfCFTA. The Google blog documented her "meeting in Mombasa with representatives from the African Union Commission, AfCFTA Secretariat, UN Economic Commission for Africa, private sector, and think tanks". This appears to be one of the earliest documented direct interactions between Google personnel and AfCFTA Secretariat officials.[^3]

Separately, Boa-Guehe and **Charles Murito** (then Google's Government Affairs Director for Africa) were photographed meeting with AU Commissioner **Albert Muchanga** and AU Special Advisor **Jean Bertrand Azapmo** to "discuss partnerships for small and medium businesses, women's entrepreneurship, and digital trade".[^3]

## Timeline of Key Milestones

### 2022: Foundation Building

- **February 2022**: Sundar Pichai addresses the African Union Summit, commits $1 billion to Africa's digital transformation, and references the AfCFTA as a trade catalyst.[^2][^1]
- **Early 2022**: Pren-Tsilya Boa-Guehe joins Google in the newly created pan-African institutions role, based in Nairobi.[^3]
- **March 2022**: Boa-Guehe travels to Mombasa for AfCFTA policy discussions with representatives of the AfCFTA Secretariat, AUC, ECA, and others—one of the first documented direct Google–AfCFTA engagements.[^3]
- **2022**: Google Hustle Academy launches, supporting SMEs across Africa. By end of 2022, it has reached thousands of small businesses.[^10][^11]
- **December 2022**: The U.S.–Africa Leaders Summit takes place; Google reaffirms its Africa commitment. Separately, the U.S. signs a formal MOU with the AfCFTA Secretariat (not involving Google, but relevant context).[^12][^10]

### 2024: Deepening Institutional Relationships

- **February 2024**: Google (represented by VP **Doron Avni**) signs a formal MOU with the UN Economic Commission for Africa (ECA) on digital transformation, startups, computer science education, and cybersecurity. This MOU, while not directly with the AfCFTA Secretariat, was aligned with AfCFTA goals and the AU Digital Transformation Strategy.[^7][^13]
- **February 2024**: The AfCFTA Digital Trade Protocol is adopted by state parties at the AU level, providing the policy framework that would later underpin the Google–AfCFTA collaboration on digital trade skills.[^14]

### 2025: Formal Programme Launch

- **April 2025**: Qhala, in partnership with the AfCFTA Secretariat and Google, convenes over 45 policymakers and tech stakeholders for a workshop on digital trade and regulatory sandboxes. **Pren-Tsilya Boa-Guehe** represented Google at the event.[^15]
- **June 2025**: The inaugural AfCFTA Digital Trade &amp; Services Forum is held. Google Hustle Academy delivered sessions on e-commerce, and the AfCFTA Secretariat presented on the Protocol on Digital Trade.[^16]
- **Late September/October 2025**: The AfCFTA Digital Inclusion &amp; Entrepreneurship Programme (ADIEP) is formally announced. Boa-Guehe publicly celebrates the launch, and a virtual launch event is scheduled for October 2, 2025.[^17][^4]
- **November 17, 2025**: ADIEP is publicly launched. The programme, powered by Google Hustle Academy and managed by UpSkill Universe, targets 7,500 African SMEs across 19 AfCFTA member states with training in AI, cloud tools, and cross-border digital trade. Secretary-General **Wamkele Mene** and Google's **Charles Murito** both make public statements.[^8][^6][^9]
- **November 2025 – June 2026**: Training runs across 25 cohorts in English, French, Arabic, and Portuguese.[^9][^8]

### 2026: Broader AUC-Google Agreement

- **February 17, 2026**: The African Union Commission (not the AfCFTA Secretariat specifically) signs a formal MOU with Google. The agreement was signed by AUC Commissioner **Lerato D. Mataboge** and Google's **Charles Njenga Murito** at AU Headquarters in Addis Ababa. The MOU covers AI infrastructure, talent development, research and innovation, MSME ecosystem support, and AI governance frameworks. While this is an AUC-level agreement (not AfCFTA Secretariat-specific), it provides an overarching institutional framework that encompasses work aligned with AfCFTA goals.[^5][^18]
- **February 2026**: The ADIEP programme expands to Nigeria, with the AfCFTA Secretariat and Google Hustle Academy actively promoting enrolment.[^19][^20]

## Formal Agreements: What Actually Exists

This is a critical distinction that the publicly available record makes clear:

### No Standalone Google–AfCFTA Secretariat MOU

Despite extensive searching, **no publicly available MOU or formal bilateral agreement between Google and the AfCFTA Secretariat has been documented**. The AfCFTA Secretariat has signed formal MOUs with numerous partners—the United States (December 2022), UNDP (2021), UNCTAD (2022), the ECA (February 2025), the UK government (2021), Australia (2025), Zenith Bank (2024), and AGRA (2026) —but no equivalent public document exists for Google.[^21][^22][^23][^24][^25][^26][^27][^12]

### What Does Exist

1. **AUC–Google MOU (February 17, 2026)**: A formal Memorandum of Understanding between the broader African Union Commission and Google, signed by Commissioner Lerato D. Mataboge and Charles Murito. This covers AI, digital infrastructure, talent, and MSME ecosystems continent-wide. The AfCFTA Secretariat is an autonomous entity within the AU system, and this MOU provides a general institutional umbrella.[^18][^5]

2. **ECA–Google MOU (February 2024)**: Signed between the UN Economic Commission for Africa and Google, covering startup development, computer science education, and cybersecurity—broadly aligned with AfCFTA digital trade goals.[^13][^7]

3. **ADIEP Programme Partnership (2025)**: The AfCFTA Digital Inclusion &amp; Entrepreneurship Programme appears to be structured as a programmatic collaboration rather than a treaty-level agreement. It involves the AfCFTA Secretariat, Google (through Hustle Academy), and UpSkill Universe as the delivery partner. The exact legal form of this arrangement has not been publicly disclosed, but press coverage describes it as a "partnership" and "collaboration" rather than referencing a formal signed agreement.[^6][^8][^9]

***

## Context: Why This Matters

The absence of a standalone formal agreement between Google and the AfCFTA Secretariat is notable but not unusual. Many technology companies engage with international institutions through programme-level partnerships, grants, or joint initiatives rather than MOUs. The AfCFTA Secretariat tends to sign formal MOUs with sovereign governments and major multilateral organisations, while private-sector engagement often takes the form of programmatic cooperation.

The broader AUC–Google MOU signed in February 2026 may now serve as a de facto institutional framework for Google's engagement with AfCFTA-related activities, given that the AfCFTA Secretariat operates within the AU system. However, the AfCFTA Secretariat is legally an "autonomous entity" within the AU structure, so the AUC MOU does not automatically extend to it.[^28][^5]

It is possible that a specific Google–AfCFTA agreement exists but has not been made public, or that the partnership operates under informal arrangements or programme-specific contracts not disclosed to the public.
